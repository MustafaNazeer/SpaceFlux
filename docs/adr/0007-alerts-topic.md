# ADR 0007: The alerts topic

* **Status:** accepted
* **Date:** 2026-09-30

## Context

`risk-engine` turns raw feed events into two kinds of results, and `query-api` stores and serves them:

* **Space weather levels.** `StormRules` derives a G, R, or S level from one `raw.swpc` record at a time, with the thresholds, fields, and comparisons in [docs/risk/space-weather-scales.md](../risk/space-weather-scales.md). A level is derived mechanically from one measurement and is not SWPC's own issued scale level; Section 4 of that note fixes how a level may be presented.
* **Close approaches.** A screening run propagates the watchlist and the catalog over a 7 day window and reports every approach within 5 km, together with what it covered and the objects and pairs it did not screen ([docs/risk/orbital-conventions.md](../risk/orbital-conventions.md) Section 3).

Both reach the dashboard through `query-api`, so the contract has to carry everything a reader needs to read a result correctly: that a level is derived and from which measurement, the value with its unit, band or channel, and satellite, the time the value describes, whether it is an estimate, and when the data was actually fetched, so that replayed or archived data is never shown as current, and enough on a schedule that a quiet series can be told apart from a stale one. A quiet reading, a gap in the data, and a level must stay different things. No field name, enumeration value, or description may tell anyone to act or imply that conditions are safe, because SpaceFlux is a public data demonstration and not a warning or collision avoidance service.

Facts from the scales note that shape the design:

* Kp values are estimates that can be revised ([ADR 0006](0006-kp-record-identity.md)); a revision can raise, lower, or remove the level of an interval that was already reported.
* GOES values come one satellite at a time. Values from two satellites are never merged, but when SWPC changes the primary satellite, the new satellite's series takes over and the old one is **ended** (Section 5.4). The new satellite's records for the past hours arrive at once, and only the newest of them sets the current state.
* Some values do not set a level (Section 5.1): among others, an X-ray flux below the floor (including the zeros SWPC writes during eclipses), above 0.2 W m-2, or a would be "none" value on the edge of a zero run. The values just before a zero run are only known to be on its edge once the zeros arrive, so they are derived on arrival and restated afterwards (Section 5.2).
* A series is in "no data" when its newest record was rejected, or when the newest `time_tag` of any of its records with a usable `time_tag` is older than the series' age limit, measured against the clock (Section 5.3).
* The R and S levels are per 1 minute and per 5 minute sample. One event per sample would repeat an unchanged level every minute, but publishing only changes leaves a consumer unable to tell a long quiet period from a feed that stopped, so the current state is repeated when newer records arrive and by a timer when nothing else went out (Section 5.3).
* Delivery is at least once, so every consumer needs an identity to deduplicate on.

## Decision

1. **One topic, three event kinds.** `alerts` carries every result. The envelope has `schema_version`, `kind`, `rules_version`, `event_id`, and `produced_at`, plus exactly one object named after the kind, in the same style as `product` and `record` on `raw.swpc`:

   | `kind` | Object | One event is |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | `space_weather_level` | `space_weather_level` | A change of one space weather series, a correction of one of its earlier samples or intervals, or a periodic refresh of its current state |
   | `close_approach` | `close_approach` | One approach within the report distance found by one screening run |
   | `screening_run` | `screening_run` | What one screening run covered and every object or pair it did not screen, unless the summary was cut to its size budget, in which case `omitted` counts the entries left out (decision 15) |

   The topic name sits next to SWPC's own `swpc.alerts` product on `raw.swpc`, but the two are unrelated: an `alerts` event is a result SpaceFlux derived, never an SWPC alert, watch, or warning, and no surface words it as an SWPC ALERT message.

   The schema is [`schemas/alerts/v1.schema.json`](../../schemas/alerts/v1.schema.json), under the rules of [ADR 0002](0002-event-schemas-and-serialization.md). The field by field contract is in [docs/data/topics.md](../data/topics.md#alerts).

2. **Space weather events are published per change of state, per series, with a periodic refresh, and worded per sample.** A series is the G series (Kp has no satellite), or the R or S series of one GOES satellite. `trigger` says why an event exists:
   * `level_change`: the series' current state differs from its previous state, including a change into or out of "no data" or "ended" and the first state known after a start. Samples are read in `time_tag` order, each can change the state, and a new sample in the same state as the one before publishes nothing.
   * `revision`: SWPC sent a different Kp value for an interval already seen. Every revision is published, whether or not its level changed (a revision within one level changes the value shown next to it), including a revision to "none". G only. When the revised interval is the newest one, the revision also sets the current state, and it is the only event published for it: no separate `level_change` follows. A revision of an older interval replaces only that interval's state. A revision to a value the rules reject (for example Kp 9.5, Section 5.1) is still a `revision`: its state is `no_data` with `no_data_reason` `rejected` and `no_data_since` the interval start, and it carries `previous_state` (with `previous_derived_level` when that was a level), `time_tag`, `interval_start`, `interval_end`, `fetched_at`, `source_url`, and `freshness_reference`, but no value; the record is also dead lettered with `check` `rule`. Its identity is the ordinary sample identity. When it revises the newest interval, it sets the current state to `no_data`, like any other revision of the newest interval. Allowing this shape changed the version 1 schema after its first draft; as with `ended`, that is acceptable only because no producer has published against version 1 yet.
   * `restatement`: a sample that was published as "none" turns out to lie within the edge before a zero run and is restated as "no data" (Section 5.2). SWPC's value did not change; a later record did. Published only when the restated sample had an event of its own, which includes a refresh on arrival that carried it as the newest record, because consumers store R states per `sample_time` and only then does a published state change; the start of the new "no data" span moves back to the first restated edge sample after the last level inside the leading edge in either case, so a "no data" span never covers a published level. R only. A restatement replaces that sample's state and never sets the current state.
   * `refresh`: the state did not change. A current series (not ended, within its age limit) repeats its current state with its newest record, that record's value, `xray_class` where it applies, and `freshness_reference`. It is published when newer records arrive for the series without changing its state, and by a timer fallback only when nothing went out for the series within its interval; the intervals are in Section 5.3. A state change is still published at once. A refresh has `state` `level` or `none` only, because a series in "no data" or "ended" does not go stale.

   `revision` and `restatement` are separate values rather than one general correction because their causes differ and surfaces word them differently: SWPC changed its value, or SpaceFlux re-read an unchanged value in the light of a later record. A consumer applies both the same way, by replacing the state of that interval or sample. Neither ever withdraws a level at R1 or above by the edge rule, since an edge value at a level keeps it. Restating on arrival rather than holding values back keeps R levels from arriving one poll later (Section 5.2 weighs both).

   Each event describes one sample or one 3 hour interval, never an event with a start and an end.

3. **Four states.** `state` is `level` (with `derived_level` 1 to 5), `none` (a valid value below level 1), `no_data`, or `ended`. `no_data` carries `no_data_reason` (`rejected`, `zero_run_edge`, or `age_limit`) and `no_data_since`. `ended` applies to R and S only and carries the satellite that took over (`ended_by_satellite`) and `no_data_since`. An ended series reads as "no data" (`derived_label` `no data`) but is no longer the current series of its scale, so a dashboard neither keeps its last level nor shows it in "no data" indefinitely; that is why it is a state of its own and not a reason code. Adding `ended` after the first draft of this contract changed the `state` enumeration inside version 1; that is allowed here only because nothing has been published against version 1 yet. The same grounds cover every other change made to version 1 after its first draft: `refresh` added to `trigger`, `unit` made required, and the `no_data` shape of a rejected Kp revision (decision 2). After the first release, the ADR 0002 rules apply in full.

4. **Every space weather event says what it was derived from.** The field is `derived_level`, never a name that reads as an issued or forecast level. `derived_from` names the measurement in words, and the event carries `value`, `unit`, `band` (R) or `channel` (S), and `satellite` (R and S only). G carries `interval_start` and `interval_end` in UTC, R and S carry `sample_time` and `averaging_period_s` (60 and 300), and SWPC's own `time_tag` is kept as sent. `estimated` is `true` for every G event. `fetched_at` and `source_url` are copied from the `raw.swpc` event the value came from.

5. **Freshness.** `freshness_reference` is the newest `time_tag`, read as UTC, of any record of the series with a usable `time_tag` when the event was produced, rejected values, zeros and edge values included (Sections 5.1 and 5.3); for G that is the newest `interval_start`, the start of the interval, because Section 5.3 measures a series' age from its newest `time_tag` and a Kp `time_tag` is the start of its interval (Section 1.3). A consumer shows the series as current only while the clock minus `freshness_reference` is within the series' age limit in Section 5.3; this contract does not repeat the limits. `risk-engine` also publishes the `no_data` event with reason `age_limit` when the clock passes that limit. Because age is measured against the clock and not against `fetched_at`, archived or replayed data never reads as current: a level derived from it is published as a statement about its own sample and is followed at once by `no_data`. The `refresh` events of decision 2 keep `freshness_reference` moving on the consumer's side while a series is current and quiet.

6. **X-ray class.** An R event in state `level` from GOES-16 or a later satellite may carry `xray_class`: M or X, truncated to one decimal, with the X number continuing past 10 (for example `M1.0`, `X3.9`, `X20.0`), derived as Section 2.4 defines. The schema allows it on no other event, and ties it to `derived_level`: R1 is M1.0 to M4.9, R2 M5.0 to M9.9, R3 X1.0 to X9.9, R4 X10.0 to X19.9, and R5 X20.0 and above, since Section 2.4 makes class and level agree at every threshold. It describes one 1 minute value, not a flare. In the same way `derived_label` is tied to `derived_level`, and a G `value` cannot exceed 9.005, the largest Kp Section 5.1 accepts.

7. **Rejected values produce "no data", never "none".** A value rejected under Section 5.1 sets no level and is dead lettered (decision 13). An X-ray `flux` of exactly 0 is the exception: it is SWPC's observed marker for a missing measurement (Section 5.2), not corrupt data, so it is not dead lettered; it is rejected in the same sense, sets the series to `no_data` with reason `rejected` when it is the newest record, and is counted by `risk-engine`. If a rejected or edge value is the newest record of its series, the series goes to `no_data` with reason `rejected` (or `zero_run_edge` for an edge value after a zero run, which is known to be on the edge as it arrives). The rules themselves and their bounds, including the edge width, are the scales note's; this contract only names the reason. An edge value that reads as "no data" is not dead lettered: the record itself is valid, and only its reading changes.

8. **Primary satellite switch** (Section 5.4). When a satellite's records reach or pass the newest `time_tag` of another satellite's series of the same scale, `risk-engine` publishes `ended` for the old series and the new series' state. The records a new or resumed series receives in one response (they share one `fetched_at`) are read as one batch: only the newest sets the state and can produce a `level_change` event. The older records of the batch are history; they are not published on `alerts`, since publishing them would present past hours as new. They stay available from `raw.swpc`.

9. **Kafka keys.**

   | Event kind | Key | Ordering it buys |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | `space_weather_level` | The scale: `space_weather.G`, `space_weather.R`, or `space_weather.S` | Every event of one scale, across its satellites, is read in the order it was produced. A consumer applying them one at a time sees an ended series and the series that replaced it in that order, never two series both current, and reads a revision or restatement after the event it corrects |
   | `close_approach` and `screening_run` | The run's `run_id` | Every event of one run lands on one partition. The run's approaches are published first and its summary last, so reading the summary means every approach of the run has been read |

   The same conditions as on the raw topics apply: a fixed partition count, producer idempotence, and one at a time processing per partition. Runs are not ordered against each other by the key; a consumer orders them by `window_start`.

10. **Rules version.** Every event carries `rules_version`, an integer I raise by hand whenever a threshold, a rule, or a screening setting changes: anything in the scales note or the orbital conventions that can change a derived level, a rejection, or a screening result, and any change to the station stack list (`risk-engine/src/main/resources/screening/stacks.json`) or to the watchlist (`risk-engine/src/main/resources/screening/watchlist.json`). It is part of every `event_id` and of `run_id`. Without it, reprocessing `raw.swpc` or `raw.gp` under changed rules would write events under the identities of the old ones, and consumers would drop the corrected results as duplicates.

11. **Event identity.** `event_id` is built only from fields of the event, so a producer retry or a reprocessing run under the same rules writes the same identity for the same event, and consumers drop an identity they have already applied. `<v>` is `rules_version`:

    | Event | `event_id` |
    | --- | --- |
    | `space_weather_level` from a sample (`level_change` or `revision`) | `space_weather_level/<v>/<scale>/<satellite or ->/<time_tag>/<fetched_at>` |
    | `space_weather_level`, `restatement` | `space_weather_level/<v>/R/<satellite>/<time_tag>/<fetched_at>/restated` |
    | `space_weather_level`, `refresh` on arrival | `space_weather_level/<v>/<scale>/<satellite or ->/<time_tag>/<fetched_at>/refresh` |
    | `space_weather_level`, `refresh` by timer | `space_weather_level/<v>/<scale>/<satellite or ->/<time_tag>/<fetched_at>/refresh/<timer_refresh_at>` |
    | `space_weather_level`, `no_data` | `space_weather_level/<v>/<scale>/<satellite or ->/no_data/<no_data_reason>/<no_data_since>` |
    | `space_weather_level`, `ended` | `space_weather_level/<v>/<scale>/<satellite>/ended/<ended_by_satellite>/<no_data_since>` |
    | `close_approach` | `close_approach/<v>/<run_id>/<watchlist number>/<other number>/<time_of_closest_approach>` |
    | `screening_run` | `screening_run/<v>/<run_id>` |

    `fetched_at` is part of the sample identity because a Kp value can be revised and later revised back: the second revision back carries the same `time_tag` and value as an earlier event, and only its later fetch time tells the two apart. A GOES record's identity has no value in it, and every consumer of `raw.swpc` drops an identity it has already processed ([docs/data/topics.md](../data/topics.md#deduplication-1)), so a GOES record republished with a later fetch time never reaches the rules; a Kp record republished with an unchanged value is not a revision and changes no state. A restated sample keeps the `time_tag` and `fetched_at` of its original event, since its cause is a later record, so its identity adds the suffix `restated`. A sample is restated at most once: a restatement only turns "none" into "no data", and the pipeline never re-reads a GOES identity it has already processed, so a restated sample is never derived again. (Whether SWPC revises GOES values is undocumented, as [ADR 0005](0005-swpc-polling-and-error-handling.md) records; this is a property of the pipeline, not of the data.) A refresh on arrival is identified by the newest record it carries, with the suffix `refresh`; one record gives either a state change or a refresh, never both. A timer refresh repeats a record an earlier event already carried, so its identity adds the timer tick, `timer_refresh_at`. **Timer refreshes are not reproducible on replay:** they depend on when the clock ticked and on what else had gone out, so a reprocessing run writes a different set of them, or none for data already past its age limit. They carry no new information, so a consumer loses nothing by treating them as it treats any other refresh. Every other identity is reproducible. A "no data" spell is identified by its reason and `no_data_since`, the time it starts, and an "ended" spell by `ended_by_satellite` and `no_data_since`, so two spells of different cause that start at the same minute stay distinct; a catch up batch can, for example, start a `rejected` spell at the same minute an `age_limit` spell was dated. A series past its age limit never leaves "no data" on a record that is itself still past the limit (scales note, Section 5.3, rule 1 takes precedence), so an `age_limit` spell is never published twice. A pair can have more than one approach in a window, so the time of closest approach is part of the approach identity.

12. **Screening runs.** A run starts its window at the `fetched_at` of the newest `raw.gp` event among the element sets it uses (orbital conventions, Section 3.2), and `run_id` is that time, written as in `window_start`, a slash, and `rules_version`, for example `2026-09-29T05:20:09Z/1`. No input fetch time is added: the window start already is one, a new element set batch has its own `fetched_at`, and a rerun over the same input under the same rules is the same run and should deduplicate. The rules version is added so a rerun under new rules is a new run. Within one process lifetime of the risk engine, and only with one risk engine instance reading every `raw.gp` partition, a `run_id` once written is never produced again with other content: element sets that arrive late for a run already written wait for the next newer fetch, which starts a new run. The set of written runs is held in memory and the screening consumer rebuilds its state from the start of `raw.gp` on every start ([ADR 0008](0008-java-kafka-client-and-schema-validator.md), decision 6), so after a restart the replay can rebuild a run under a `run_id` already written, with other content: an element set that arrived late for that run, after it was written, is included on the replay. Two instances sharing the screening consumer group over a `raw.gp` with more than one partition would each screen a partial catalog under the same `run_id`. Consumers keep the first event for each `event_id` (decision 11). A replayed run can also carry `close_approach` events with new `event_id` values, which the summary a consumer kept first does not list; under the completeness rule below they are not shown as part of the run. This is a known limitation, to be revisited when the risk engine scales out. `input_fetched_at` stays in the summary as its own field, equal to `window_start` in this version, because it says where the data came from rather than how the window was chosen; a run's results are stale 24 hours after its `window_start`, and always after its window end, under the staleness rule for screening results in the [orbital conventions](../risk/orbital-conventions.md). Every `close_approach` carries its `run_id`; the `screening_run` event carries the same `run_id`, `approach_count`, and the `event_id` of the approaches (every one, unless the summary is cut to its size budget, decision 15), and that fixes what the run is. When `omitted.approach_event_ids` is 0, or `omitted` is absent, the run is exactly the `close_approach` events whose `event_id` the kept summary lists: a `close_approach` with that `run_id` whose `event_id` the kept summary does not list belongs to a rebuilt run (above) and is not shown as part of this one. Only when ids were cut does a consumer count instead: it holds the whole run once it holds `approach_count` distinct approaches with that `run_id`, and it cannot then tell an approach of a rebuilt run from an unlisted approach of the kept one. Each run replaces the previous one: the current set of approaches is the newest run whose summary has arrived and all of whose approaches, by that rule, have been received. The summary carries every result field of the screening code (coverage counts, suppressed pairs with their sampled separations, rejected objects, objects not screened, element sets with an epoch after the window start, differing copies), cut to a size budget only when it would not otherwise fit (decision 15), and is published even when a run finds nothing. No approaches means only that no searched pair's SGP4 propagation came within the report distance: objects not in the input, rejected, not screened, or in a suppressed pair are not covered, and every distance is uncertain by kilometres ([orbital conventions](../risk/orbital-conventions.md), Section 5).

13. **Rule rejections are dead lettered on the topic they were read from.** When the risk engine rejects a schema valid `raw.swpc` record under a rule of the scales note, it writes a dead letter to `raw.swpc.dlq` with `service` `risk-engine`, `stage` `validate`, and the new optional field `check` set to `rule`. That includes an X-ray flux above 0 but below the floor and one above 0.2 W m-2; an X-ray flux of exactly 0 is not dead lettered (decision 7). A topic schema failure, in `ingest` or in a consumer, sets `check` to `schema`. `check` is set only when one of those two checks rejected the payload; it is absent when the payload never reached either check (the UTF-8 and record size dead letters in ingest), when neither check rejected it (a record that passed its topic schema but failed ingest's identity parse, for example an EPOCH that matches the pattern but is not a real date, or an element set that passes its schema but that `risk-engine` cannot turn into an orbit, dead lettered to `raw.gp.dlq`), and on dead letters written before the field existed. Element sets are checked the same way: an element set whose `EPOCH` is more than 5 minutes after its `fetched_at`, or an event whose `fetched_at` is more than 5 minutes after the risk engine's clock, is a rule dead letter on `raw.gp.dlq` (orbital conventions, Section 2.4). An element set the risk engine cannot turn into an orbit is one whose fields cannot be converted into an Orekit `TLE` object under the checks of the orbital conventions, Section 2.4, for example a `MEAN_MOTION` at or below 0, an `ECCENTRICITY` at or above 1, or a `NORAD_CAT_ID` of 0, all of which the `raw.gp` schema allows. The risk engine never formats text TLE lines, so a value that only the fixed width text lines cannot hold is no reason to dead letter an element set, and same epoch copies are compared field by field rather than by their lines (orbital conventions, Section 2.4, "Copies with the held epoch"). `alerts.dlq` receives `alerts` events that fail validation at the producer or at a consumer. Adding `check` as an optional field keeps dead letter version 1, which is already in use; a new `stage` value would have been a change to an enumeration, which ADR 0002 makes a new major version.

14. **Reason codes of the screening summary are open lists.** `role` and the event kinds are enumerated, because a new value there changes what an event means. The suppression mechanism, rejection code, and not screened kind are lower case codes with their known values documented in [docs/data/topics.md](../data/topics.md#alerts). A consumer shows an unknown code with its readable reason instead of rejecting the event, so adding a code does not need a new major version.

15. **The screening summary is bounded.** A summary is one Kafka record, and the `alerts` producer refuses a record over `max.request.size`, 1,048,576 bytes by default in `kafka-clients` 4.2.2; a refused write is retried every 5 seconds, so an oversized summary would be retried without end. Three limits keep it under that:

    * **Names are capped at 64 Unicode code points.** The cap is applied when the risk engine reads an element set, so the held state, copy comparisons and every event use the capped name. A longer name is cut to its first 61 code points followed by `...`. CelesTrak's three line format gives a name 24 characters, and the longest name in the recorded stations group is 20. `raw.gp` keeps no length limit on `OBJECT_NAME`: a limit there would be a new major version under ADR 0002, and it would dead letter a whole element set, and drop the object from screening, over its name.
    * **At most 3 differing copies of the held epoch are held per catalog number.** Further differing records for that number and epoch are counted, not held, and do not restart the 30 second quiet period; the summary lists the count per catalog number in `differing_copies_over_cap`. `ingest` skips an element set whose (`NORAD_CAT_ID`, `EPOCH`) pair it already published, so a same epoch copy normally appears only after an `ingest` restart finds changed content.
    * **The summary has a budget of 900,000 bytes**, the record size threshold the dead letter writers in `ingest` and the risk engine already use, which leaves 148,576 bytes under the producer limit for the key and record overhead. Over budget, entries are cut from the end of the lists in a fixed order, each list emptied before the next is touched: `differing_copies`, `differing_copies_over_cap`, `epoch_after_start`, `approach_event_ids`, `suppressed`, `not_screened`, `rejected`. Bookkeeping about duplicate records goes first; approach ids next, since `approach_count`, `run_id` and the run's partition still let a consumer count its approaches; the per object record of what was not screened goes last. Within `rejected` and `not_screened` the watchlist role entries come first, so catalog role entries are cut first. The object `omitted` counts the entries cut from each list, and is written on every run, all zero when nothing was cut. The budget is checked with `produced_at` counted at its longest form, 30 characters, so where the cut falls depends only on the run's input and never on when it was produced. Every `close_approach` event is still published, whatever the summary keeps.

    These changes amend `alerts` version 1 in place rather than starting version 2. Adding `omitted` and `differing_copies_over_cap` as optional fields is compatible under ADR 0002, and the name cap only narrows what the producer writes, but cutting the lists changes what existing fields promise: `approach_count` no longer always equals the length of `approach_event_ids`, the coverage sum counts cut suppressed pairs, and not every differing copy is listed. ADR 0002 would make that a new major version. Version 1 is amended instead because no consumer of `alerts` exists yet, so no reader depends on the old promises. A summary without `omitted` was never cut.

## Alternatives considered

* **One topic per kind** (for example separate space weather and screening topics). Each schema would be simpler and each could have its own retention. Rejected for now because both kinds go to the same consumer and the same store, and the discriminator keeps one contract with one set of rules. Worth revisiting if the two need different retention.
* **One event per space weather sample.** Simplest to produce and to replay, but it repeats an unchanged level every minute for R and every 5 minutes for S, and every consumer would have to collapse the repeats itself.
* **Publishing only level changes for Kp revisions.** A revision within one level would then leave the value shown next to the level wrong.
* **One `correction` trigger for both Kp revisions and edge restatements.** Fewer values, but a surface could no longer say whether SWPC changed its value or SpaceFlux re-read an unchanged one.
* **"Ended" as a `no_data_reason`.** Keeps three states, but a consumer would have to know that this one reason, unlike the others, removes the series from being current.
* **Publishing the history records of a switch batch** with their own trigger. They would reach consumers as events about past hours arriving all at once, which the scales note rules out presenting as new; `raw.swpc` already holds them.
* **Keying space weather events per satellite** (`space_weather.R.16`). Spreads the R and S events over more keys, but a switch involves two satellites of one scale, and with separate keys a consumer could read the new series before the old one is ended, or the reverse. The volume is small either way: with refresh on arrival, each R and S series publishes about 12 events an hour (one per ingest poll), and G a few, so one partition per scale costs nothing.
* **Keying close approaches by the watchlist object's catalog number** (the key `raw.gp` uses), with the run summary keyed by `run_id`. All approaches for one watchlist object would then be read in order across runs, but the summary would sit on another partition from its approaches, so it could not mark a run as complete by its position; `approach_event_ids` would be the only way to tell.
* **`run_id` from the window start and the input fetch time together.** Redundant once the window start is defined as that fetch time.
* **No refresh, only changes.** Fewer events, but a series that stays at one state for hours would look the same as one whose feed stopped, until the age limit passes.
* **Refreshes only by timer.** Simpler, but each refresh would repeat what the records already said, late; a refresh on arrival carries the newest record as soon as it exists.
* **A timer refresh identified by its record alone.** It would share the identity of the event that first carried that record, and be dropped as a duplicate.
* **Rules identified by a hash of the settings** instead of a hand raised number. Automatic, but a rule is partly prose in the scales note and the orbital conventions, which a hash of code settings would not see.
* **A new dead letter stage for rule rejections.** Clearer to read, but a change to the `stage` enumeration, which needs dead letter version 2 and a period in which consumers accept both.
* **Identities assigned at random by the producer.** Rejected because a retry after a crash would write the same event under a new identity, which deduplication cannot catch.
* **Kafka position of the source record in the identity** (topic, partition, offset). Unique and stable, but it changes if a raw topic is ever rebuilt, and it would tie the alerts contract to broker internals.

## Consequences

* A consumer reconstructs the current state of a scale from the latest events on its key, refreshes included: the series that is not ended with the newest `freshness_reference`, shown as current only within its age limit. It stores G states by `interval_start` and R and S states by satellite and `sample_time`, taking the latest event for each as its state. After a restart of the risk engine, the first event of a series has no `previous_state` and may repeat the state a consumer already holds; that is not a change.
* A dashboard can show a derived level, its measurement, its time, its satellite, whether it is an estimate, and whether its series is stale or ended, from the events alone, without reading the raw topics.
* The age limit and the timer fallback both need a clock driven check in `risk-engine`: a series goes to "no data" when no record arrives at all, not only when a bad one does, and a current series with nothing else sent within its interval is repeated.
* Raising `rules_version` and reprocessing publishes a second set of events alongside the first. A consumer that keeps history keeps both and shows the newest rules version; one that keeps only current state is unaffected.
* The zeros SWPC writes during eclipses (runs of 62 to 66 minutes each night on the primary satellite in eclipse season, Section 5.2) are counted rather than dead lettered, so `raw.swpc.dlq` keeps only values that are genuinely suspect: a nonzero flux below the floor, a flux above 0.2 W m-2, and the other Section 5.1 rejections. The count is how an unusual number of zeros stays visible.
* Because the sample identity includes the fetch time, rebuilding `alerts` from a replay of `raw.swpc` gives the same identities, but events derived from a second fetch of the same record (after an ingest restart) are distinct events; the per change of state rule means they are only published when the state actually changed.
* The summary is bounded at 900,000 bytes (decision 15). A run over the stations group is far below it, so its lists are complete in practice; a run that does reach the budget loses list entries, never counts: `omitted` says how many entries each list lost, and the coverage counts and `approach_count` stay exact. A consumer that needs every cut entry has no way to recover it from `alerts`; if catalogs grow to where cuts are routine, the summary's shape is revisited.
